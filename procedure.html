HUMERUS

MEASUREMENT:

•    MAXIMUM LENGTH

Instruments Required: Osteometric board Anatomical Landmarks • Upper point: Most superior point of the head of humerus • Lower point: Most inferior point of the trochlea.

PROCEDURE: 

•    Place the humerus on the osteometric board with the anterior surface facing upward.
          •    Position the head of the humerus firmly against the fixed vertical end of the board.
          •    Align the shaft so that it lies straight along the board.
          •    Slide the movable vertical end of the board until it touches the lowest projecting point of the trochlea.
          •    Record the value as Maximum Length of Humerus.

PRECAUTIONS • Ensure the humerus is not tilted or slanted. • Avoid measuring up to the capitulum (incorrect). • Take two or three readings and record the average. • Measure left and right humerus separately.

•    BREADTH OF PROXIMAL EPIPHYSIS:

Instrument used: Sliding Caliper Anatomical Landmarks • Medial point: Most projecting point of the head of humerus • Lateral point: Most projecting point of the greater tubercle

PROCEDURE • Hold the humerus with the proximal end facing upward. • Identify the most lateral projecting point of the greater tubercle. • Identify the most medial projecting point of the humeral head. • Place the fixed jaw of the sliding caliper on the medial point of the head. • Place the movable jaw on the lateral point of the greater tubercle. • Ensure the caliper is held perpendicular to the long axis of the humerus. • Record it as Breadth of Proximal Epiphysis of Humerus.

PRECAUTIONS • Do not include the shaft in the measurement. • Ensure maximum breadth is taken, not oblique. • Avoid excessive pressure with calipers. • Take at least two readings and record the average.

•    BREADTH OF DISTAL EPISPHYSIS:

Instrument used: Spreading caliper Anatomical Landmarks • Medial landmark: Medial epicondyle • Lateral landmark: Lateral epicondyle

PROCEDURE: • Hold the humerus with the distal end facing upward. • Identify the most projecting point of the medial epicondyle. • Identify the most projecting point of the lateral epicondyle. • Place one tip of the spreading caliper on the medial epicondyle. • Place the other tip on the lateral epicondyle. • Adjust the caliper carefully to obtain the maximum breadth. • Ensure the caliper arms are held perpendicular to the long axis of the humerus. • Read the measurement.

Precautions • Ensure the widest distance is measured, not an oblique one. • Avoid applying excess pressure on the epicondyles. • Do not include broken or worn margins. • Take two to three readings and record the average.

•    LEAST GIRTH OF SHAFT:

Instrument Required: Measuring tape Anatomical Landmark: Least circumference of the humeral shaft (mid-shaft region).

PROCEDURE • Hold the humerus in the anatomical position. • Locate the narrowest part of the shaft (usually near the mid-shaft). • Wrap the measuring tape around this point, ensuring it fits snugly. • If using thread: wrap the thread around the shaft and mark the overlap point. • Lay the thread straight on a measuring scale and note the length. • Record the measurement as Least Girth of Shaft of Humerus.

PRECAUTIONS • Ensure the tape/thread is tight but not overlapping. • Measure at the minimum girth only, not above or below. • Avoid damaged or eroded parts of the bone. • Take two readings and record the average.

•    CONDYLO- DIPHYSICAL ANGLE

Instruments Required • Goniometer

Anatomical Landmarks • Shaft axis: Longitudinal midline of the humeral shaft • Condylar axis: Line joining medial and lateral epicondyles

Normal Range (Approximate) • Usually around 70°–85°, varying with sex, population, and laterality.

PROCEDURE • Place the humerus on a flat surface or osteometric board. • Align the shaft so that it lies straight and stable. • Determine the longitudinal axis of the shaft by visually aligning its midline. • Identify the condylar (epicondylar) axis, which is the straight line joining: • the most projecting point of the medial epicondyle, and • the most projecting point of the lateral epicondyle. • Draw or imagine both axes clearly. • Place the center of the goniometer at the point where the shaft axis meets the condylar axis. • Measure the angle between the shaft axis and the condylar axis. • Record the reading in degrees as the Condylo-Diaphysial Angle of Humerus.

PRECAUTIONS • Ensure the bone is not tilted or rotated. • Axes must be accurately identified. • Measure the angle on a horizontal plane. • Take more than one reading for accuracy.

•    HUMERAL TORSION ANGLE

Instruments Required: Goniometer

Anatomical Landmarks • Proximal axis: Axis of the head of humerus • Distal axis: Line joining medial and lateral epicondyles.

PROCEDURE • Place the humerus on a flat surface with the distal condyles resting evenly on the table. • Ensure that the line joining the medial and lateral epicondyles lies horizontally. • Identify the axis of the humeral head, usually represented by a line passing through: • the center of the head, and • the center of the shaft. • Draw or imagine this head axis. • Place the center of the goniometer at the point where the head axis intersects the horizontal plane. • Measure the angle between the head axis and the epicondylar axis. • Record the reading in degrees as the Humeral Torsion Angle.

PRECAUTIONS • Distal condyles must be in the same horizontal plane. • Avoid rotation of the shaft during measurement. • Axes must be clearly identified. • Take two readings and record the average.
